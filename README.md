**Starshard Arena** is a neon arena survival game for the desktop browser. You are the Kid — spiky hair, red headband, and a blaster almost as big as you are — alone against the Hollow: eight kinds of creature, each drawn as a character with its own face and temper. Maws that chew their way toward you ringed in teeth, Lances that lean back and flash before they lunge, Broods whose unborn children drift inside a translucent sac, one-eyed Mites, cloak-and-wisp Wraiths whose mouths open a breath before they spit, bone-plated Bulwark knights that turtle when hurt, wasps whose stingers charge light before they fire — and every fifth wave the Dreadnought, a many-eyed leviathan in counter-rotating siege rings that enrages at half health. Everything on screen is bigger now: no enemy is a coin-sized sigil, and the boss fills a third of the arena. Kills drop more than points: the Hollow leave **skill Cores** — Overdrive, Seeker Missiles, Piercing Bolts, a Nova Burst, and the Lance Beam, a continuous bar of light that replaces your bolts while it burns — that you steal by running over the wreck, mid-fight. Some Hollow arrive gilded as **named elites** — Vorath the Gilded, Granny Vex, the Pale Choir — wearing their names over their crowns, and when one falls the arena announces it: *GRANNY VEX IS DOWN*. The fight is not one place but a route: every three waves you cross into a new named **sector** — Azure Drift, Violet Deep, Ember Rift, Verdant Void — and the whole void changes its weather and light around you. A sweeping **radar** in the corner of the cockpit tracks every Hollow as a blip in its own colour, and a **pilot record** outlives every run: sorties, kills, named elites felled, and your deepest wave, kept between visits. But the swarm eats too: a Hollow that reaches your dropped shards first swallows them and grows. Chain kills into a combo to charge the screen-clearing **Starfall**, dash through danger and leave a burning ion wake, fight inside gravity **rifts** that drag friend and foe alike, and between waves spend your shards at the Forge, where every permanent upgrade — extra cannons, piercing bolts, a seeker rack — is bought with the remains of the creatures you broke, or banked toward something bigger. Every run builds a different blaster. Keyboard and mouse, procedural WebAudio sound and music, no build step and no network: the whole game is the static files in `game/`.

## Run it

```sh
node serve.ts
```

Then open http://localhost:8080 and click LAUNCH. (To package the entry instead: `mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts`.)
